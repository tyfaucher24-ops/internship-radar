Takeaways:
- Tier 1 (bulge-bracket and elite banks, MBB, top quant, FAANG) is under about 2% across sources, with banks and MBB under 1%.
- Below that tier there are no published rates, so tiers 2–5 use real signals instead: firm size, brand-list membership, metro size and remote status.

## 2. What screens matter
- **GPA:**
  - In the fall 2024 Job Outlook 2025, 47% of employers planned to screen by GPA (37% in 2023, 38.3% in 2024) — [NACE Job Outlook 2025](https://www.naceweb.org/research/reports/job-outlook/2025).
  - The spring 2025 update showed under 40% again — [NACE spring update PDF](https://www.naceweb.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/2025/publication/research-report/nace-2025-job-outlook-spring-update.pdf).
  - GPA is a real but partial screen, so it is weighted strongly where firms publish cutoffs (finance, consulting) and moderately elsewhere.
- **Internship experience:** "the most influential factor" when choosing between equal candidates, especially an internship in the employer's industry or with that employer — [NACE](https://www.naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/candidate-selection/1000). This gives relevant internships the largest experience credit, and more for selective or same-industry ones.
- **Major:** considered by 92–94% of employers; internship in the industry by 84–85%; leadership position by 67–70%; extracurriculars by about 65% — NACE spring update PDF above.
  - **School reputation:** considered by only 27–41% of employers.
  - **Part-time job while in college:** considered by 46.6% of employers that don't screen on GPA.
  - Taken together, school gets only a slight, tiered boost, and work while in school and leadership get modest credit.
- **Skills:** about 90% look for problem solving, about 80% teamwork, and at least 70% technical skills — [NACE resume attributes](https://www.naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/candidate-selection/what-are-employers-looking-for-when-reviewing-college-students-resumes).
- **Tech vs banking:** tech hiring leans on technical assessments more than school prestige; banking and consulting still lean on target schools — [Whali](https://whali.com/blog/internship-acceptance-rates-2026), [StrategyCase](https://strategycase.com/mckinsey-internship).
  - StrategyCase estimates that about 20–30% of target-school applicants get a McKinsey first round, against 5–10% of non-target applicants. These are estimates, not McKinsey figures.
  - The grader therefore weights school for IB, PE and consulting but not for software or data.

## 3. Athletes and distinctive experience
- **D1 / varsity athletes in finance:**
  - In a study of 120,306 Ivy graduates, athletes were about 5% of graduates but 7.08% of first finance jobs.
  - One more former teammate at a firm raised an athlete's odds of joining it by 193.7%.
  - Morgan Stanley and Bank of America were the most athlete-heavy employers.
  - Sources: [HBS working paper](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=69326), [Fortune, Aug 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/08/01/wall-street-recruiting-pipeline-ivy-league-sports-teams-hiring-edge/).
  - The effect works mostly through alumni networks, so it is evidence for real but modest credit in finance, sales and consulting, plus a networking action.
- **Athletes in general corporate hiring:** 828 corporate recruiters rated varsity athletes higher on teamwork and results, but lower on organization, and **were not more likely to call them for interviews** — [Journal of Amateur Sport (KU)](https://journals.ku.edu/jams/article/download/7468/12649/26940). Outside finance, sales and consulting, athletics therefore gets only a small credit.
- **Sport and leadership:** in an EY/espnW survey, 94% of C-suite women played sport, and 2 in 3 executives said a sport background positively influences a hiring decision — [EY](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-au/campaigns/athlete-programs/documents/ey-where-will-you-find-your-next-leader.pdf). This supports leadership credit, not any specific acceptance number.
- **Military, founding something, research, first-gen:** no published intern-hiring effect sizes were found.
  - Military, founding something and research get small capped credit under NACE's "leadership" and "industry experience" factors.
  - Research counts as relevant experience for science and engineering roles.
  - First-generation status gets **no grade change**: no source shows a screening effect, so stating one would be invented.

## 4. Grader weights that come from this research
| Rule | Value | Why (source above) |
|---|---|---|
| Company tier 1–5 stored per company with a reason | tier 1 = cited acceptance rate ≤2% | §1 |
| Top-grade compression | tier 1: A needs a standout (target or semi-target school or GPA ≥3.7, **and** a relevant job or internship); tier 2: A needs a relevant job or internship | §1, NACE internship factor |
| School boost | slight, tiered: target +3, semi-target +1.5 points, IB/PE/consulting only (already scored there); never a free pass | NACE school reputation 27–41%; StrategyCase target vs non-target |
| Experience credit | relevant internship counts most; selective or same-firm counts more | NACE "most influential factor" |
| Extra credit (capped at +6 points total) | D1 +3 / D2–D3 +2 / club +1 in finance, sales or consulting, about half that elsewhere; leadership +1.5; military +2; founded something +1.5; research +2 for science, engineering and data roles (+0.5 elsewhere); part-time work while in school +1 | HBS/Fortune athlete study; KU recruiter study; NACE leadership 67–70%, part-time 46.6% |
| Cap | extra credit never exceeds +6 points and cannot lift a profile over a tier ceiling | the spec requires that extra credit never outweighs GPA or internships |
| First-gen | 0 | no evidence found |
